# 02. Competitor analysis

Researched 2026-11-16 with web search and page extraction. **Traffic figures are not included.** Semrush, Ahrefs and Similarweb were not available, so "weight" below is judged from company scale, directory dominance and published claims. The worksheet in section 6 replaces those judgments with data in Phase 0.

## 1. Market map

The category has five layers. ServePoint competes directly in layer 4 and borrows interface lessons from layers 2 and 3.

| Layer | Who | What they own |
|---|---|---|
| 1. National and enterprise networks (Canada) | Canadian Process Serving Inc., Dye & Durham, Lormit Legal Support Solutions | Scale, decades in business, nationwide coverage, law firm account relationships |
| 2. US platforms | ABC Legal, Proof, Proceed Legal, Undisputed Legal, ServeNow (directory and software) | Search traffic for generic process serving terms, ordering portals, integrations, tracking, content marketing |
| 3. Modern regional specialists | Dial and File Process Servers (Toronto) | Flat-rate pricing, GPS-logged service, real-time milestone updates, thought leadership in Canadian Lawyer |
| 4. BC local operators | Expedited Motion Process Serving, Rapid Taggers, Syer Legal Services, West Coast Title Search, Matrix, TNT Process Serving, Mr. Process Server, City Wide Process Servers, Action Process Serving, WCPS West Coast Process Serving and others | Local listings (BBB shows 35 process server results near Vancouver), referrals, price points |
| 5. Corporate search providers | Ontario Business Central, West Coast Title Search, Best Search & Registry, and BC Registry itself | Fast, no-account ordering of searches |

**Read of the local market:** fragmented, listing-driven and visually dated. Most BC operators are visible through Yellow Pages, BBB and Canada411 entries more than through strong brand sites. That is the opening: a clear, modern, trust-forward site for law firms is rare here.

## 3. Patterns worth borrowing

- **Ordering as the primary action**, repeated once per screen, not eight times per page.
- **A four-step process shown as a sequence**: send documents, attempt, serve, receive proof. It is a real sequence, so numbering is legitimate here.
- **A sample proof document.** Competitors describe proof. Showing a sample (clearly marked fictional) is rarer and more convincing.
- **Published price structure.** Competitors hide prices behind quotes. Show the base price, what is included and what changes it.
- **A line that removes a worry** next to the CTA ("Same business day confirmation. Pricing confirmed before service.").
- **Matter or file reference field** in the form. Law firms bill by matter.
- **Honest limits in the FAQ.** Hague work, evasive parties, what happens when service fails.
- **Audience-based entry** ("For law firms", "Instructing from outside BC").
- **Thought leadership under a named lawyer.** Guides by the founder are the local equivalent of Dial and File's trade press.

## 4. Patterns to avoid

- Unsourced or inconsistent statistics. Counters that fail to render.
- Fake logo walls, stock handshakes, gavels, scales.
- Templated city pages with swapped place names.
- "Typical provider" tables that denigrate unnamed competitors.
- Claims ServePoint cannot back: GPS tracking, a client portal, years in business, ratings.

ServePoint's base price sits above commonly cited ranges. For a law-firm audience this can hold when the value is visible: 24 hour first attempt, a sworn affidavit, three attempts, a direct contact, a lawyer-founded firm. The pricing page must therefore show what is included next to the number. Ask the owner whether the positioning is deliberate (open question 5).
