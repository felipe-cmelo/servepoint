Sometimes personal service does not work. The person avoids it, has moved, or cannot be found at the address on file. This guide describes what the Supreme Court says about the next step and why the record of earlier attempts matters. It does not tell you whether to apply or what to ask for. Your firm decides that.

## What the court says

The Supreme Court's guidance says that if personal service is not possible, you can get a court order giving you permission to use an alternate method of service. Rule 4-4 of the Supreme Court Civil Rules governs this. The guidance gives examples of alternate methods, such as mailing the document to the person's last known address, leaving it at their home or with another adult there, or publishing an advertisement in a newspaper. The advertisement uses Form 10.

The court's family law guidance says these options are not available when you are serving documents in a family law case. Read the family page and the Family Rules for what applies there.

## What the supporting affidavit describes

The Supreme Court's guidance says your affidavit must describe when, how and where you tried to serve the person and what happened. This is why each attempt needs a date, a time, a location and the relevant details.

## How a record of attempts helps

If service cannot be completed after the included attempts, ServePoint provides an affidavit of attempted service and an update on next steps. The record of each attempt supports whatever your firm decides to do next. We do not advise on whether to apply for an order.

## Check the current rule

Rules and forms change. Read the current text of Rule 4-4 and Form 10 at BC Laws, and the court's own pages, before you rely on this guide.
