When the defendant is a company, the first question is where it can be served. This guide describes how a BC corporate search confirms the registered office the registry holds today and where to read the rule on serving a company. It does not tell you how your matter must be served. Your firm decides that.

## What the rule says

Rule 4-3 of the Supreme Court Civil Rules deals with personal service, including service on a company. The Supreme Court's own guidance on serving documents summarises it for people who are not lawyers. Read both before you decide how to serve. The text of the rule governs.

## Why confirm the registered office

An address in a contract, a letterhead or an old pleading can be out of date. A BC corporate search shows the registered office on the registry today, along with the company's status and its directors and officers. Running the search before service is attempted means the address you give the process server is current.

## The searches

ServePoint runs these BC Registry searches: company summary, director and officer search, registered office confirmation, historical search, certificate of status, and name and trade name search. You can send a company name, an incorporation number or a trade name. Results are delivered by email, typically within 2 business days.

## Search first, serve second

You can order the search and the service together on one instruction. We confirm the registered office first, then attempt service. Each attempt is recorded with its date, time and location.
